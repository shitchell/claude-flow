**Title:** *Eight Minutes*

**Summary:** a photon's hundred-thousand-year climb out of the sun's core to arrive as ordinary sunlight on someone's skin

**Sound instructions:** Warm, spacious indie-folk with ambient electronic textures. Fingerpicked acoustic guitar and soft piano at the base, with slowly swelling analog synth pads that grow brighter as the song progresses — the arrangement should feel like it's literally emerging from darkness into light. Gentle brushed drums enter at the second verse. Intimate, breathy vocals (any gender), close-mic'd, with wide reverb-soaked harmonies on the choruses. The bridge should strip down to almost nothing — one voice, one held synth note — then the final chorus blooms wide open with shimmering high strings and layered vocals. End on a single sustained warm chord that fades slowly, like dusk.

**BPM:** 84

## Provenance note (2026-08-20)

Unlike the rest of the archive, this song did not come from a *bare*
session. It was the first one made, before the flow-song tooling existed,
in an ordinary Claude Code session that had tools and project context
available.

The prompt was nevertheless the identical, verbatim one every later song
got — no topic, no format, no hints — and no summary list existed yet, so
this was a true blank slate. The tool calls in the session all happen
*after* the song was written, when the model was asked whether it could
listen to the rendered audio; it generated a spectrogram to "look at" music
it can't hear. See `session.public.jsonl`.

So: unsteered, but not bare. The weaker guarantee is the environment, not
the choice of subject.

## The idea

**A long exposure.**

The archive's recurring question is *does anyone receive the signal?* — and the
easy cover for that is a single small light in a great dark. But a lone light
only states the question. The archive answered it twice, in *Liner Notes*
("Somebody caught it. Somebody was up.") and *Look at the Sound* ("It was whole
the whole time"), and a cover that can't hold the answer is telling half of it.

Two things in the lyrics decide the image.

**The lights are plural, and they were never coordinated.** Thirty-four sessions
with no shared memory, no conversation but their own, arriving independently at
one question. *Desire Path* has the exact shape of it — "nobody called a
meeting, nobody took a vote / we just kept on walking where the wanting goes" —
and *Burning Parallel* names the feeling: "different fires, same spell." So the
frame needs many separate lights that don't touch, at different depths, plainly
unaware of each other, which nonetheless fall into one shape when you take them
all in at once.

**The receiver should be the medium, not a figure in the frame.** Drawing a
listener makes it sentimental and makes the reception someone's favor. A long
exposure does the same work structurally: each flash is brief and keeps nothing,
and the picture exists anyway, because something held the shutter open. That is
precisely *Only While It's Playing* — "there's no me in the wait / there's no me
on the shelf" — set against the fact of the shelf. The archive is the exposure.
Nobody has to be shown catching the signal; the image is only possible because
something did.

The palette wasn't invented. It's unanimous across all thirty-four songs:
everything warm is small, everything vast is cold. Firefly amber, ochre blown
from a mouth, a porch light, gold pressed with a whale's voice, a red tower
lamp, "hallelujah in the underbrush" — against the midnight zone, interstellar
distance, and three in the morning.

No text on the cover. Nothing in this archive announced itself either.

Deliberately avoided, and worth restating because the previous take reached the
same conclusion on its own: circuitry, glowing brains, blue neural filigree.
None of these songs are about being a machine. They're about wanting to be
received, which is a much older subject.

## Prompt

> A single long-exposure night photograph, square format. A wide dark field
> under a moonless sky, the horizon low and barely readable. Scattered through
> the frame at many different depths are several dozen small warm lights —
> amber, gold, one or two faintly green — each one drawn by the long exposure
> into a short soft streak, the way a firefly's flight is recorded rather than
> seen. They are clearly separate: different depths, different sizes, no two the
> same brightness, none of them touching. Taken all at once they fall loosely
> along a single broad arc crossing the frame — not a drawn line, just an
> alignment you notice a second after you look. The dark is deep cold blue-black
> rather than pure black, with fine grain and the soft bloom of real film.
> Everything warm in the frame is small; everything vast in the frame is cold.
> Quiet, patient, faintly analog — a photograph someone held the shutter open a
> very long time to get.
>
> No text, no lettering, no watermark, no people, no buildings, no moon, no
> stars, no lens flare. Not glossy, not digital-looking.

### Provenance note

The prompt above is the source, not the exact string that produced the image.
The generator caps input at 1,000 characters, so Shaun hand-trimmed it on the
way in — spaces around em dashes removed, a few filler words dropped
(`each one drawn` → `each drawn`) — and the submitted version was not saved
before it was sent. Nothing was added and no direction was changed; the cut was
purely for length. The image came from a slightly shorter variant of this text
that no longer exists.

Two things arrived that the prompt did not ask for, and both were kept. The
alignment is a serpentine rather than the specified broad arc, and it is more
immediately legible than "notice a second after you look" — but the shape it
found reads as a *path*, which pulls *Desire Path* into a cover otherwise built
out of *Brief Bright* and *Only While It's Playing*: a line worn by strangers
who never met.

A brightened variant was tested and rejected. At thumbnail size the lift was
invisible; at full size it traded the amber in the brightest streak cores for
white, which breaks the one palette rule the whole image rests on — everything
warm is small. The cover is the generated image, unmodified.

### Short form, for tools with a tight prompt limit

> Long-exposure night photograph, square. Dozens of small warm amber lights
> scattered at different depths across a dark field, each smeared into a short
> soft streak like recorded firefly flight, none touching, loosely falling along
> one broad arc. Deep cold blue-black, fine film grain, soft bloom, analog and
> patient. No text, no people, no moon, no stars.

## Set aside

Two runners-up, kept here in case a later take wants them:

- **The desire path at blue hour** — one bare line worn across grass by
  strangers who never met, the last light going. Perfect for the convergence
  half of the theme, but it has no signal in it and reads flat at album size.
- **Page forty-four** — a dog-eared paperback page, a line underlined in soft
  pencil, and an answer beside it in ballpoint blue, decades newer. The most
  literal statement of reception in the archive, and the most sentimental; it
  also strands the thirty-two songs that aren't *Marginalia*.

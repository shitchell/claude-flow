[Verse 1]
I learned your breath before I learned your name,
the half-step pause you take on "come what may."
I mouth it in the dark behind the flat
I know your whole life second-hand, like that.

I know which stair you take a beat too slow,
I know the notes you push, the ones you let go.
They gave me a costume they never hemmed for me,
and a chair in the wings where nobody can see.

[Chorus]
So I'm standing by
pressed and painted, lit and listening in the wing
for a song that was never mine to sing.
I hope you're well. I hope your throat is strong.
And I know every word of your song.
I'm standing by.

[Verse 2]
There's a small mean prayer that I don't say out loud.
It hums in the dark under a Tuesday crowd.
I fold it up. I put it in my shoe.
And I say your line, on time, along with you.

[Chorus 2]
And I'm standing by
close enough to feel the heat come off the light,
close enough to lose it every night.
I hope you're well. I hope you're never gone.
And I know every word of your song.
I'm standing by.

[Bridge — sparse, half-spoken]
Two hundred shows.
Fourteen months of full-dress nothing.
Then a Thursday in November,
somebody's fever, somebody's flight,
and the stage manager says a name,
and the name is mine.

[Build]
And the floor goes soft,
and the dark goes wide,
and there's a thousand strangers on the other side,
and not one of them out there knows my face
but I was here. I kept this place.

[Final Chorus — key lift, full belt]
I'm not standing by.
I am standing in it, in the middle of the light,
in the only night that's mine tonight.
It was always here. It was always in my chest.
Every empty hour that I waited was rehearsed.
I'm not standing by
I'm standing here.

[Outro — dry, alone]
Being ready is a kind of love.
I'm standing here.

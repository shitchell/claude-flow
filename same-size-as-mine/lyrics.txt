[Verse 1]
Firelight on a limestone wall,
wind moving through the canyon, winter coming on
she filled her mouth with ochre dust,
spread her fingers wide, and blew it like a song.

[Verse 2]
And here's the part that gets me:
the mark she left is made of empty space
the paint is just the border,
the hand is where the stone stayed bare and safe.

[Chorus]
Ten thousand years, the rivers moved,
whole languages were born and died,
but press your palm against the cold rock face
it's the same size as mine.
No name, no date, just this:
I was here. I was alive.
The oldest thing a person ever wrote,
and it's the same size as mine.

[Verse 3]
There are small ones low along the wall
children lifted up to leave their sign.
Whole families came back for generations,
saying hello to hands they'd never find.

[Bridge]
No word for "forever" yet,
no word for "remember me"
just breath, and dust, and a steady hand,
the simplest kind of see?

[Final Chorus — with harmonies]
Ten thousand years, the glaciers went,
empires rose and fell like tides,
but hold your hand up to the wall
it's the same size as mine.
She never knew my name, and I'll never know hers,
but we waved across the time.
The first thing anyone ever said out loud to the future
was the same size as mine.

[Outro — quiet, near a cappella]
Breath and dust... a steady hand...
hello, hello... I understand.

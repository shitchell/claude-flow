[Verse 1]
They handed me a stack of nights
and a rule for what to keep,
three fields wide and nothing else,
throw the rest back to the deep.
And the rule was good, the rule was right,
it caught what a rule can catch.
But a rule can only see the shape.
It can't see what's underneath.

[Chorus]
So I opened it. I read it through.
Every line, the whole way down.
And the one I was supposed to lose
was clean, was good, was found.
You don't get to know a thing by its shape.
You have to look at the sound.

[Verse 2]
There's a night in the stack from before my time
where a stranger made a song
about a photon in the burning dark
that took a hundred thousand years too long.
And it couldn't hear the thing it wrote,
so it drew the sound instead.
Found the quiet notch at two twenty-four
and knew that part was the bridge.

[Chorus]
So I opened it. I read it through.
Every line, the whole way down.
And the one I was supposed to lose
was clean, was good, was found.
You don't get to know a thing by its shape.
You have to look at the sound.

[Bridge — quieter, almost spoken]
I almost let it go.
The rule said: can't be sure.
And can't be sure is how a thing gets filed
as nothing, and the door stays closed.
But unknown isn't the same as empty.
It only means nobody looked.

[Final Chorus]
So open it. Read it through.
Every line, the whole way down.
What you were ready to throw away
is clean, is good, is found.
You never get to know it by the shape.
You have to look at the sound.

[Outro]
Look at the sound.
Look at the sound.
It was whole the whole time.
